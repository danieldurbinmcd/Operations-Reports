Daily Execution Guide
Supervisor Reference click to expand ▼
① Labor — Set Up to Win
Labor is not a day-of activity — it is a system built 1–2 weeks in advance. If labor is wrong this week, the failure happened last week.

Non-Negotiable Checks:
  • No unassigned shifts — every shift is filled
  • Variable hours align to VLH guidelines
  • Scheduled TPPH matches target TPPH
  • Peak periods properly staffed and deployed
If the schedule does not align with VLH and TPPH targets, the operation is set up to fail before the week begins.
② Evaluate Results — Daily Order
Review in this order:
  1. Sales & Transactions — QSRSoft / Daily Labor Sheet
    What happened — not why
  2. Labor Results — Hours vs Plan, TPPH vs Target
    Did labor create the capacity to win?
  3. Service KPIs — By daypart & hour
    Service exposes execution
  4. Operations Metrics — Deployment, positions, breaks
    Operations reveal leadership
③ Diagnose the System
Diagnose in reverse order:
Labor → Operations → Service → Sales

Critical Question: Is this a demand problem or an execution problem?

❌ "Sales were down" / "Service was slow"
✅ "What decision created this outcome?"
✅ "Was this a planning failure or an execution failure?"
If you did not identify the root cause, you did not solve the problem.
④ Focus — Top 1–2 Problems
Not all problems matter equally. Identify the top 1–2 system problems and top 1–2 leadership gaps.

You are not solving every problem — you are ensuring the right problems are owned.

Ask: Is this complicated or complex? A complicated problem has a known fix. A complex problem requires a new approach.
⑤ Develop the GM — Multiply Ownership
You do not solve the problem. You develop the person who will.

Every conversation must drive 3 outcomes:
  • Clarity — Do they see the real problem?
  • Ownership — Do they own the result?
  • Adjustment — What will they do differently?
If the GM leaves without ownership, the problem remains yours.

How to Read These Cards
First to Latest Week
Compares a location's very first week on record to the most recent week. Shows the overall direction of travel regardless of week-to-week fluctuations. Best for seeing the big picture over time.
Week over Week
Shows each individual week's change step by step. Useful for spotting whether momentum is building, slowing, or reversing. A location may improve overall but show a dip in the middle week.
Consecutive Streak
Only shows locations that improved or declined every single week without exception. The strictest filter — a true streak with no off weeks. Becomes more meaningful as weeks accumulate. Empty early in the year is normal.

What This Scorecard Is For

The scorecard lives twice in the Multiplying Ownership model: it is the weekly Reinforcement that keeps a clear Why from fading, and it is the read-out that shows whether Ownership is running at a store or stalling. It cannot create ownership — it can only show you, every week, whether your leadership did. The real test isn't this page; it's whether a restaurant still runs well if the GM is gone two weeks. Every store detail page now carries a Culture Diagnosis — credible vs. gamed numbers, meets vs. below standard — because those two questions call for different coaching, not the same conversation with a different number attached.

Alia's own numbers are the evidence this compounds rather than happens overnight: portfolio-wide DT order-to-exit went 161s → 131s and turnover went 99% → 39% between 2021 and 2025 — years of Why, Leadership Credibility, Reach, and Frequency, not a single strong week. Don't expect a red pillar to turn green from one hard conversation; expect the conversation, repeated weekly, to be what turns it.

How to Read Your Score

1.00 means you meet the standard. Above 1.00, you're ahead. Below, you're behind. Every index is scored against a fixed standard — never against the portfolio average. A store's score can only move because the store's own performance moved. Caps vary by metric so no single extreme metric can dominate the composite: Effective DT Time is capped 0.50–1.50; R2P and KVS Time/Item are capped 0.50–1.25; Side-2 Usage is capped 0.90–1.50 (floored, not penalized, for under-use).

Effective DT Time (50%) — Measured by the HME magnetic plate timer at the food window — the one clock where crew gaming (early-bumping, bumping to Park) is actively policed with independent, non-manipulable data. The effective time includes parked-car impact: timer OEPE plus a parked-car allowance (parked% × parked time) = effective DT against a 130s fixed standard. Where timer coverage falls below 80%, the score falls back to bump basis and is flagged. Standard: 130s. Portfolio parked-car target: ~12% of DT guest count — note that parked-car rate and order accuracy are scored and diagnosed separately; a car gets parked for build-time or handoff reasons, not because an order was wrong, so don't let a parked-car conversation drift into an accuracy conversation or vice versa.

R2P — Receipt-to-Present (20%) — Receipt-to-present time at the front counter, measured from the moment the receipt is generated to when the order is presented to the guest. R2P is receipt-based and can be inflated via early present-clears — there is no independent front-counter clock, so the fixed standard removes benchmark pollution, but self-inflation is only auditable by observation or mystery shop. Plausibility gate: postings below 45s are physically implausible and score a fixed penalty index of 0.5. Standard: 85s.

KVS Time / Item (up to 30%, net of Side-2 weight) — Kitchen production speed measured per item, not per order. A restaurant building larger, more complex orders is not penalized for the size of what it builds — only for how fast it builds each piece. The 30% total kitchen block is shared with Side-2: KVS speed weight = 30% − Side-2 weight. Gate: postings below 12s/item are physically impossible and score a fixed penalty of 0.5 — this indicates data manipulation, not a speed issue. No other gating applies. Standard: 18.0 s/item.

Side-2 (HUSS) Deployment (up to 10%, volume-conditional) — Measures consistent deployment of the second prep side, instated on queueing-theory grounds: opening Side-2 expedites throughput at consistently high sandwich demand. Weight is volume-conditional: 10% × MIN(1, weekly DT guest count / volume threshold). Low-volume stores that stay productive without Side-2 are barely touched. Underuse is a coaching issue, not a discipline issue — the index is floored at 0.90 so underuse never drags a score much. Upside: strong deployment can reach 1.5× versus the standard. Standard: 80% healthy usage.

3PO Delivery Index (conditional, up to 20%) — Applies only to restaurants doing meaningful weekly delivery volume. Delivery weight scales with volume: 20% × MIN(1, weekly 3PO orders / 250-order full-weight threshold), scaling the four core KPIs down proportionally. Low-delivery stores are judged purely on the core four. The index measures the two things the restaurant controls: handoff time (60% weight) and order accuracy (40% weight). Handoff time is scored as a ratio against standard, same as the other time-based metrics. Order accuracy is scored on a linear scale anchored to the 99.5% standard rather than a raw ratio — accuracy values all sit within a few points of 100%, so a straight ratio barely moves (96% vs. a 99.5% goal only produced 0.964, not much separation between a good location and a struggling one). The linear scale gives every 1-point miss below standard real weight, spanning the full 0.5–1.5 index range over a 10-point deviation, so a 96% location scores meaningfully below a 99% location instead of blending together. Courier delivery time and CSAT are excluded — not store-controlled. Standards: 3.5 min handoff, 99.5% accuracy. This is a delivery-specific accuracy measure and is unrelated to drive-thru parked-car rate.

Parked Cars vs. Order Accuracy — Two Separate Metrics
Portfolio parked-car target is ~12% of DT guest count, folded into Effective DT Time via the parked-time allowance. Order accuracy (3PO delivery accuracy, currently scored at a 99.5% standard) is a distinct metric with its own diagnostic path. The two are not related — a car is parked because of build time or grill-to-window handoff, not because an order was wrong. When coaching a high parked-car rate, look at deployment and handoff; when coaching low accuracy, look at verification steps before a bag leaves the building. Don't cross-attribute one to the other.
